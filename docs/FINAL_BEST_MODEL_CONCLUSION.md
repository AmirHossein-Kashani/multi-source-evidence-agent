# Final Conclusion: Best AMG-RAG Model Configuration

This project evaluated successive AMG-RAG configurations using the same ten adverse drug reaction questions and the same underlying language model, **Qwen2.5-14B**, served locally through Ollama. The phases therefore compare retrieval and evidence-reasoning strategies rather than different foundation models. Because a central objective of this work is to **find, preserve, and explain contradictory evidence**, the final conclusion is that **Phase 13 is our best and most important research configuration**. Phase 11 remains the strongest precision-oriented comparator, but it does not provide Phase 13's contradiction-resolution capability.

Phase 13 combines wide, graph-guided PubMed retrieval with ClinPGx/PharmGKB grounding, expanded open-access full-text retrieval, paragraph-level evidence digestion, a rule-based evidence ledger, and a dedicated contradiction-resolution layer. It produced the broadest and deepest evidence set: **145 gene–drug pairs**, **23.8 references per question**, **17 papers read in full**, and **70 relevant paragraphs cited**. In the research-weighted scorecard, where contradiction handling carries the greatest weight, Phase 13 ranked first with a composite score of **4.44**, substantially ahead of Phase 11 at 3.31.

Its main contribution is that it moves beyond simply counting disagreements. Earlier ledger phases could flag `literature_internal` or `curated_vs_literature` conflicts, but they treated evidence from different scientific levels as directly comparable. For example, an in-vitro null result could be counted as contradicting a clinical association. Phase 13 classifies evidence as curated, clinical, mechanistic, or narrative and permits a contradiction only between comparable evidence tiers. This approach generated **29 mechanistic-only downgrades**, preventing indirect laboratory evidence from being presented as a clinical refutation.

Phase 13 also normalizes claims by population, cancer type, endpoint, dose context, and direction. This allowed the system to explain three apparently contradictory associations as context-dependent: TPMT–cisplatin findings differed by hearing-loss endpoint, SLC28A3–anthracycline findings differed between children and adults, and GSTM1–doxorubicin findings differed by cancer type. These examples demonstrate the project's intended capability: not only detecting that publications disagree, but identifying **why** their conclusions differ. Phase 13 classified 22 pairs as genuinely conflicting, three as context-dependent, and 120 as insufficient for resolution.

The high number of *INSUFFICIENT* results is a limitation, but it is also scientifically honest. For 83% of candidate pairs, fewer than two comparable clinical sources were available. Rather than forcing agreement or contradiction from weak or mismatched evidence, the system explicitly reports that the evidence cannot support a conclusion. Phase 13 still requires improvement: its on-topic retrieval precision was 49%, it missed the CYP2D6–doxorubicin false-premise test, half of its detected conflicts lacked a fully extracted explanatory axis, and its confidence calibration has not been independently benchmarked.

Our final recommendation is therefore to adopt **Phase 13 as the best model configuration for the project's core research goal of contradiction discovery and resolution**. Phase 11 should be retained as a parallel precision and clinical-safety control because it achieved the highest on-topic precision (65%), passed the false-premise test, and is the only advanced phase with measured confidence calibration. The preferred future system is a hybrid: Phase 13's wide retrieval and comparability-aware contradiction layer combined with Phase 11's precise free-form searches and premise checking. Further work should improve context extraction, distinguish curated-only evidence from truly insufficient evidence, make the `CONSISTENT` status reachable, normalize gene symbols, benchmark Phase 13's confidence scores, and require citations in every final answer. With these improvements, the system can provide a rigorous map of where pharmacogenomic evidence agrees, conflicts, or remains unresolved.
