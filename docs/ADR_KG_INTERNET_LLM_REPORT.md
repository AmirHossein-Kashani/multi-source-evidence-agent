# Influence of Parametric LLM Knowledge, Knowledge-Graph Grounding, and Internet Access in an ADR Question-Answering System

## Background and objective

Adverse drug reaction (ADR) questions are difficult for a language model because a medically plausible answer is not necessarily an evidence-supported answer. A model may remember common drug mechanisms and familiar genes from training, yet confuse biological plausibility with a demonstrated drug–gene–phenotype association. Curated pharmacogenomic resources provide more precise associations but may lag behind recent publications. Live literature retrieval can address this delay, although it introduces irrelevant, weak, or contradictory evidence. Our experiments therefore examined the distinct contributions of three information sources in AMG-RAG: (1) knowledge already encoded in the language model, (2) a curated pharmacogenomic knowledge graph (KG), and (3) internet access to current biomedical literature.

All configurations used the same locally hosted Qwen2.5-14B model. The principal evaluation set contained ten clinical ADR questions, mainly concerning cisplatin ototoxicity or neuropathy and anthracycline cardiotoxicity. The earliest offline and PubMed baselines contained nine of these questions. Because the ADR questions have no reference answers, we did not use BERTScore or conventional answer accuracy. Instead, we measured evidence volume, retrieval relevance, whether genes in the answer were present in retrieved evidence, whether ClinPGx linked each gene to the relevant drug, performance on a deliberately false premise, and—among advanced phases—the number and type of contradictory claims. The small sample and absence of gold answers mean that the results are descriptive rather than statistically conclusive.

## Experimental comparison

The offline baseline disabled external retrieval. Its KG was constructed only from concepts extracted from the question; consequently, its biomedical content primarily reflected the model's parametric knowledge. A second baseline enabled the original PubMed/Wikipedia retrieval used by AMG-RAG. Phase 3 replaced this with an offline KG derived from ClinPGx/PharmGKB tables, including drug–gene–phenotype relationships, variants, evidence levels, and curation status. Later phases combined the curated KG with PubMed abstracts, open-access full text, improved graph-guided queries, an evidence ledger, and finally a context-aware contradiction-resolution layer.

| Configuration | Main information source | Key observed result |
|---|---|---|
| Offline baseline | LLM knowledge plus question-derived graph | Only 61% of named genes were linked to the relevant drug in ClinPGx; false premise missed |
| Original internet baseline | LLM plus PubMed/Wikipedia | ClinPGx-valid gene rate fell to 50%; false premise still missed |
| Phase 3 | Curated ClinPGx/PharmGKB KG, no internet | 95% ClinPGx-valid genes and 100% provenance-grounded genes; 8.5 references/question |
| Phase 4 | Curated KG plus cited/raw-query PubMed | 95% ClinPGx-valid and 100% grounded; 22.6 references/question; false premise detected |
| Phase 7 | Curated KG plus broad open search/full text | Greatest early reach (26.9 references/question), but only 23% on-topic and false premise missed |
| Phase 11 | KG, focused live search, full text, evidence ledger | Highest retrieval precision (65%) and false premise detected |
| Phase 13 | Wide KG-guided search plus contradiction resolution | 145 gene–drug pairs, 17 full-text papers and 70 cited paragraphs; three context-dependent conflicts resolved |

## What each information source contributed

The LLM alone generally produced coherent mechanisms and clinically fluent answers, but often selected genes that were plausible rather than specifically supported. For cisplatin ototoxicity, the offline answers proposed SLC22A8, ABCC3, SLC15A4, SLC2A1 and ABCB1. For doxorubicin cardiotoxicity, they emphasized ABCB1 and GSTP1. Across the baseline answers, only 61% of the named genes were linked to the relevant drug in ClinPGx. This illustrates a central weakness of parametric knowledge: it is useful for language generation, broad mechanistic interpretation and hypothesis formation, but it provides neither explicit provenance nor a reliable distinction between a remembered association and a biologically plausible extrapolation.

The curated KG produced the clearest categorical improvement. Phase 3 raised the ClinPGx-valid proportion from 61% to 95%, and every gene in its answers was traceable to retrieved evidence. It replaced plausible transporter guesses with curated associations and exact variants. For example, it surfaced GSTM1 and TPMT for cisplatin ototoxicity and SLC28A3 rs7853758 for doxorubicin cardiotoxicity; the latter carries PharmGKB level 2B evidence and did not appear in either ungrounded baseline. The KG also preserved evidence levels and ambiguity labels, allowing the system to qualify moderate or disputed associations. However, KG grounding did not solve every reasoning problem: Phase 3 still accepted the false CYP2D6–doxorubicin premise, and a curated database alone cannot fully represent new studies or explain why studies disagree.

Internet access added breadth, recency, and the ability to validate or challenge curated claims, but its benefit depended strongly on search quality. Phase 4 increased evidence volume from 8.5 to 22.6 references per question without reducing the 95% ClinPGx-valid rate. It recovered ACYP2, a literature-supported cisplatin-ototoxicity locus that Phase 3 missed, and correctly rejected the false CYP2D6–doxorubicin association. Broad open search in Phases 6–7 retrieved still more literature and more full text, but only 20–23% of papers mentioned both the relevant drug and adverse effect. Noise from generic queries such as “cancer” or “tinnitus” crowded out the corrective curated evidence, causing the false-premise error to return. Thus, more internet evidence was not automatically safer or more accurate.

Graph-constrained query construction substantially improved internet retrieval. Combining related KG terms with Boolean conjunctions raised the on-topic rate to 52–54%, while model-authored focused queries and the evidence ledger reached 65% in Phase 11. Phase 11 retrieved fewer references than the broad phases (13.0 per question) but offered the best precision and correctly handled the false premise. This result suggests that the KG contributes twice: first as a trusted evidence source and second as a structure for forming more discriminative literature searches.

The widest configuration, Phase 13, was most useful for the research objective of contradiction discovery. It surfaced 145 gene–drug pairs, processed 17 full-text papers, and cited 70 relevant paragraphs. Its resolution layer separated curated, clinical, mechanistic, and narrative evidence and prevented 29 mechanistic-only findings from being treated as direct clinical refutations. It identified three apparently conflicting relationships as context-dependent: TPMT–cisplatin differed by hearing-loss endpoint, SLC28A3–anthracycline differed between pediatric and adult populations, and GSTM1–doxorubicin differed by cancer type. Nevertheless, 120 of 145 pairs (83%) remained insufficient because fewer than two comparable clinical sources were available; 22 were conflicting, and only three were resolved as context-dependent. Phase 13 also had lower on-topic precision (49%) and missed the false-premise test. It is therefore the strongest discovery configuration, not the safest stand-alone clinical configuration.

## Interpretation and conclusion

Our findings do not support choosing one knowledge source in isolation. Parametric LLM knowledge provides fluent synthesis and useful mechanistic priors, but it can turn plausibility into unsupported specificity. The curated KG provides the largest improvement in drug–gene validity, exact variant identification, provenance, and evidence qualification. Internet retrieval contributes current studies, missing biomarkers, full-text context, and evidence needed to identify contradictions; however, unconstrained access can introduce enough noise to reverse a correct safety judgment.

The preferred architecture is consequently hybrid. For precision-oriented or clinically sensitive use, the evidence favors Phase 11: curated grounding, focused KG-guided literature search, an evidence ledger, and explicit premise checking. For systematic review and hypothesis generation, Phase 13 is preferable because it maximizes coverage and explains some disagreements by population, endpoint, and disease context. A future unified system should combine Phase 13's comparability-aware contradiction analysis with Phase 11's focused retrieval and premise checks, require a citation for every clinical claim, normalize gene and variant names, and abstain when evidence is insufficient. The main experimental conclusion is that the KG supplies reliability, controlled internet access supplies currency and scientific context, and the LLM supplies synthesis; robust ADR reasoning emerges from their constrained interaction rather than from any component alone.
